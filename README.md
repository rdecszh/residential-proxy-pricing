## What "free trial" actually means for residential proxies

The phrase covers at least four very different things, and providers rarely clarify which one they mean.

**Credit-based trials.** You get a small balance and spend it on whatever product you want. Bright Data works this way: $2 in initial trial credit, plus another $5 after adding a payment method, with a 30-day window on the limited trial. The card is for account verification and isn't charged at that stage, and the option isn't available in every country.

**Card-required trials that auto-convert.** Decodo's three-day trial is the clearest example: a payment method is required before activation, you pick a paid plan up front, and when the three days end that plan starts billing unless you cancelled. Traffic-based plans get 100 MB; IP-based plans get 100 MB plus up to 10 IPs. Worth knowing: the normal 14-day money-back guarantee doesn't apply once you've activated a free trial.

**Permanent free tiers.** Webshare offers 10 shared datacenter proxies and up to 1 GB of bandwidth a month with no card, forever. Oxylabs runs a separate free datacenter plan: 5 US IPs, 5 GB shared across them, 20 concurrent sessions, no card required. Neither is residential, which matters.

**Community promo codes.** This is where a lot of residential providers actually live. Instead of a self-serve trial, they hand out small codes through forums, Telegram channels, and affiliate communities. 9Proxy does more of this than most.

The pattern worth internalizing: residential IP pools are expensive to run, so nobody gives away meaningful residential volume through an instant public button. If a trial appears instantly and needs no verification, it's almost always datacenter. That's not a knock on datacenter proxies — it's just not the same product.

## IP-based or GB-based? Pick the trial that matches your job

9Proxy runs two separate products, and they behave differently enough that testing the wrong one tells you nothing useful.

**Residential Proxy by IPs** charges per IP with unlimited bandwidth. An IP is only deducted when you forward it to a local port, and unused IPs never sit in a countdown — they stay in your balance. Once activated, an IP stays online anywhere from a few hours to roughly 24 hours, which is normal for residential but worth planning around. You need the desktop app (Windows, macOS, Linux), authentication runs through local port forwarding with optional proxy authentication, and rotation is handled by Auto Refresh or Auto Rotation on a schedule.

**Residential Proxy by GB** charges by traffic and lets you generate unlimited endpoints — no per-IP activation, no IP counting. Sessions can be sticky or rotating, authentication is username/password or IP whitelisting, and everything runs from the dashboard without installing anything. Targeting goes to country, state, city, ZIP, or ISP. Validity is 180 days, unlimited on Enterprise.

Which to request for a trial:

- Session-persistent work — logged-in accounts, carts, multi-account management, sites that flag IP churn — test the IP model.
- High-rotation work — broad scraping, ad verification, SERP and price checks — test the GB model.

If you're genuinely unsure, the GB model is the safer first test because you can point it at your real targets in an afternoon without setting up an app.

## What to measure once you have access

Don't spend a trial measuring speed against a homepage. Test the thing you're actually going to run, and log these:

- **Success rate on your specific targets.** Aggregate numbers like the 97% success rate and roughly 1,300 ms average response time listed by third-party directories are useful context, not a prediction. Your targets decide.
- **IP lifespan against your session length.** Residential IPs die naturally. If your workflow needs a 30-minute session and the IP survives three hours, fine. If it needs six hours, that's a design problem.
- **Geo-targeting accuracy.** Confirm the exit IP actually resolves to the city or ZIP you asked for, not just the country.
- **Rotation behavior in sticky mode.** Watch whether sessions drop earlier than configured when you push concurrency.
- **Block and CAPTCHA rates on the sites you care about.** This is the whole point. Everything else is secondary.

Independent ratings are worth a glance but shouldn't drive the decision.
